# Experiments

Alternatives tried after the config was frozen. Each has a plan written before its first run, its own results and spend, and changes nothing in the submitted system or its scores.

| # | Experiment | Question | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [Cascade classifier](cascade/README.md) | Can a cheap embedding stage handle the easy lines and escalate only uncertain ones to the LLM? | Same accuracy as LLM only, but it escalated 81% of receipt lines (9% cheaper) and 99% of real ones, and it turned one uncertain real line into a false flag |
| 2 | [Decision-model verifier](verifier/README.md) | Can a second model's yes/no check on restricted lines remove false flags without clearing real violations? | Run with Baseten's Mammoth-1 (Jev needs paid credits). It confirmed all 21 real violations on the receipts, but on CORD it backed the worst false flag (*Rhum) and doubted the one real violation, so neither variant met the bar |
| 3 | [Policy text to config](policy_config/README.md) | Can an LLM pipeline fill the standardized config from the policy text, so the unchanged engine reaches the same decisions? | Every number, quote and reference kind came out exactly right, but a few design fields (when a rule triggers, its scope) differed; in every variant one changed a decision or stopped the engine. The config tests failed on every variant, but one decision-changing error passed them and showed only end to end. Works as a draft generator behind the tests, an end-to-end check and a human review |
| 4 | [Policy knowledge graph for retrieval](graph_retrieval/README.md) | Would a graph extracted from the policy text retrieve the rules each receipt needs as well as the structured index, and how do BM25 and embeddings compare? | The extracted graph matched the index (every needed rule, same decisions, 10.0 against 9.5 rules a receipt), but a constructed receipt exposed a missing trigger. BM25 and embeddings at top 10 got 18 of 28 receipts right |
| 5 | [The scored receipts reworded](paraphrase/README.md) | Is 65 / 65 partly the prompt recognising these exact item names? | No sign by the bar set in advance: 64 / 65 items and 27 / 28 receipts with every line reworded, all four reworded traps right. The miss is cooking wine (Shaoxing called food, 4 times of 4), the one trap the brief names and already the closest call |
| 6 | [Semantic first stage, then the model at temperature 0](semantic_first/README.md) | Can anchors decide the clear, safe lines and the model at temperature 0 take the rest, keeping every result? | The model at temperature 0 alone passed every bar and beat the submitted classifier on the dev set and CORD. The semantic stage decided 30% of lines (72 of 72 right on the project's own sets) but failed its bar: two restricted-flag errors on CORD lines with unsure labels, where the model made the same call. Only 3 of 32 receipts avoid the model call |
| 7 | [Local semantic lookup first, then the model](semantic_v2/README.md) | Can a local, repeatable lookup decide the clear, safe lines and the model the rest, as safely as main, on fresh receipts too? | Safe (58/58 right) and exactly repeatable, but too cautious: 6% of real lines, against a 20% bar, so it fails. Learning from reviewed receipts lifted that to 67% and 45 of 100 receipts with no model call, but then missed a real violation (makgeolli decided as food) |
| 8 | [Recognise first, generalise carefully, at scale](semantic_v3/README.md) | Streamed over 400 fresh receipts with learning after every 50, does the lookup take a growing share without calling a restricted item safe? | Yes on safety and accuracy: 0 restricted items called safe, lookup 509/511 right, 1,123 lines right against main's 1,115. It grew from 14% to 56% and 54% of lines, with a third or more of receipts needing no model call; it fails only the "never falls" bar, by 1.6 points |
| 9 | [A stricter same-item rule for memory](semantic_v31/README.md) | Does requiring shared words as well as similarity keep the lookup safe and useful on 200 fresh receipts? | Safe (122/122, nothing restricted called safe), but 21% of lines against a 35% bar, because one mislabelled pair forced the threshold to exact text. Version 3 stays: 41% of its memory decisions are resemblance, yet it was the most accurate arm (515 against the model's 505) |
| 10 | [Same-item rule, tolerant calibration](semantic_v32/README.md) | On CORD's last 200 fresh receipts, is the same-item rule safe and useful enough to replace the pipeline's memory? | No. It decided 3.6% of lines: a blocking rule meant for a high threshold blocks nearly everything at 0.665. The pipeline's own lookup called a skincare product food (via "Cream [L]") and missed one more violation than the model (5 against 4) |
| 11 | [Version 3.3 on look-alike traps](semantic_v33/README.md) | With same-item memory, blocking back at 0.855, a second vote from the library and a restricted word list, is the lookup safe on 66 restricted look-alikes and still useful? | No, narrowly: one trap called safe after more learning ("Makgeolli Original" via "ORIGINAL") and 33.5% of lines decided against 35%. The pipeline's own lookup called three alcoholic drinks food that the model got right, so it is unsafe as it stands |
